Homeowners looking for an electrician usually want three things before they call: what you do, where you work, and how to reach you when something has failed. A useful site makes those obvious on a phone.

## Say what kind of electrical work you want

"Electrician" is a start. The jobs are not the same. Fault finding, switchboard upgrades, lighting, new-build wiring, and EV charger installs attract different searches and different customers.

Name the services you want to be hired for, and give the important ones their own page. A page about switchboard upgrades can say who it is for, what is included, and which suburbs you cover. That is more useful than a single list of every job you have ever done.

If you are weighing up the structure, the [electrician website page](/trades/electrician-websites) covers the same idea from the design side.

## Put the phone where a thumb can reach it

A lot of electrical enquiries are urgent. The number should be in the header, tappable on a phone, and repeated near the end of each service page. A short form is useful for quotes that are not urgent. It should not be the only way to get in touch.

Also say how callouts work. If you take after-hours faults, say so. If you do not, say that too. People would rather know than wait on a form that nobody sees until morning.

## Show the area you actually cover

"Servicing New Zealand" is rarely true for a small crew. Name the cities, towns, or suburbs you travel to. A homeowner in a different region can then move on, and someone nearby can see they are in the right place.

Mention the area on the home page and again on the service pages. That is the honest version of local search: say where you work, in words a person would use.

## Use your own work, not stock photos

A switchboard, a finished kitchen light, or an EV charger on a driveway tells a visitor the work is real. Generic electrical stock photos do not. Even a small set of your own jobs is better than a large gallery of pictures that could belong to anyone.

If you do not have photos yet, start with a few clear shots of recent work and add to them. A site can launch without a huge gallery.

## What belongs on the first version

A small electrician site can work with:

1. A home page that says you are an electrician and names your area.
2. Pages for the services you want more of.
3. A contact path with click-to-call and a short form.
4. Hours, and how urgent faults are handled.
5. A few photos of finished work.

That is a different job from a large custom web application. [Published pricing](/pricing) is there so you can see the size of site before you ask for anything.
