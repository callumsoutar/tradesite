Plenty of trade businesses stay busy from repeat customers and referrals. A website is not a legal requirement, and it is not magic. It is worth it when people who do not know you yet are searching for the work you do.

## When a site is a poor use of money

Skip it, or wait, if:

- Almost every job comes from builders, property managers, or customers you already know.
- You are booked out and do not want new enquiries.
- You cannot answer the phone or the form. A site that promises a call-back and then goes quiet does more harm than no site.

A Facebook page or a Google Business Profile can be enough in that situation. They are free to start, and customers already look there.

## When a site starts to pay for itself

A site earns its place when strangers need to decide whether to call you. That is common for plumbers, electricians, painters, roofers, landscapers, and builders who want work outside their current circle.

Those visitors usually check three things: the services, the area, and a way to make contact. If that information only lives inside a social app, you are hoping they will keep tapping. A page of your own can state it in one place, on a phone, without an account.

[Websites for tradies](/websites-for-tradies) sets out what that page needs to do. It is a short list, not a marketing funnel.

## A Google listing and a website are not the same thing

A Google Business Profile helps you appear on Maps, with reviews, hours, and a call button. Keep that up to date. It does not replace a site if you offer several kinds of work. Maps gives you a short description. A site can give a homeowner a page about hot water cylinders, or switchboards, or a specific type of build.

Link the profile to the site, and link the site back to the profile. They support each other.

## What "having a website" should mean

A parked domain, or a page that says "coming soon", does not help anyone call you. The useful version names the trade, the services you want, and the towns you cover, and it loads properly on a phone.
