More customers, for a trade business, usually means more of the right phone calls. The reliable ways to get them are unglamorous: be easy to find, be easy to trust, and answer when people try to hire you.

## Start with the customers you already have

Referrals are still how a lot of New Zealand tradies stay busy. Make that easy. Leave a card, send a short message after the job, and ask happy customers to mention you when a neighbour needs the same work. If you have a Google Business Profile, ask them to leave the review there. A handful of specific reviews beats a new logo.

If you are already turning work away, fix the schedule before you spend money on more enquiries.

## Be findable where people already look

Homeowners search Google, Maps, and sometimes Facebook. Claim the Google Business Profile for the business, use the real business name, and keep the phone number, hours, and suburbs up to date. Pick categories that match the work, such as electrician or plumber, rather than a vague "contractor" label if a tighter one exists.

That profile and a website do different jobs. Maps is the listing. The site is where you explain the work. [Do tradies need a website?](/blog/do-tradies-need-a-website) covers when the second one is worth it.

## Make the website easy to act on

If you have a site, or you are about to, check it the way a customer would:

1. Can you tell the trade and the area in the first screen?
2. Are the services you want more of named on their own pages?
3. Can you tap the phone number without hunting?
4. Do the photos look like your jobs?

[What a tradie website needs](/websites-for-tradies) is that list in more detail. Trade pages, such as [plumber websites](/trades/plumber-websites), show how it changes when the call is often urgent.

A redesign that adds animation and hides the number will not bring more work. A clear page about the service you want, in the towns you cover, can.

## Answer the enquiry

This sounds obvious and it is where a lot of leads die. If the form sends mail nobody checks, or the mobile goes to a full voicemail, the site is doing its job and the business is not. Decide who answers, and what happens after hours.

## See it before you buy a bigger site

You do not need a large site to test this. A short one, with the right services and a real phone number, is enough to learn whether new customers can find you.
