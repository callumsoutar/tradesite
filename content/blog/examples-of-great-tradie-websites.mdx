Searching for "best tradie websites" turns up a lot of agency galleries. Some of those sites are polished. Many of them would still be hard to use if the power was out and you needed a number. Use examples to study the structure, then judge them the way a customer would.

## What a useful example actually shows

Open the site on your phone and look for four things:

- The trade and the location are obvious without scrolling through a slogan.
- The services you might hire are named, not buried in a paragraph about "quality workmanship".
- There is a phone number you can tap.
- The photos look like real jobs. If every image could belong to any company in any country, the example is teaching the wrong lesson.

Speed matters too. If the page stutters while it loads a full-screen video, it is a poor model for a business that lives on calls.

## How the concepts on this site are meant to be read

The [work page](/portfolio) shows concept designs, not client projects. Each one is a fictional business with its own palette and layout, so an electrician's site does not look like a landscaper's.

They are examples of emphasis:

- [Electricians](/trades/electrician-websites) need services such as fault finding and switchboards to be easy to find, with the phone close at hand.
- [Plumbers](/trades/plumber-websites) are often called for something that is already going wrong, so the first screen has to be calm and fast.
- Builders and landscapers have more room to show finished work, because the customer is usually choosing, not dealing with an emergency.

Use them to decide what your own first screen should say. They are not templates to copy line for line.

## What to ignore in other people's sites

Awards, stock photos of handshakes, and a slider of slogans do not help a homeowner choose. Neither does a page that lists every suburb in the country if you only work in one region. That kind of example is common because it is easy to sell. It is a weak model for a small crew.

Also be careful with review widgets that you cannot stand behind. If you do not have reviews yet, do not invent them. A clear description of the work is more trustworthy than five stars with no names.

## A better test than a gallery

Ask for a draft of your own business and open it on the phone you actually use. If a stranger could tell what you do, where you work, and how to call, the example has done its job.
